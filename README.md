# The Bicycle & the Light · a 3D exploration

**Open it:** https://csasse123.github.io/bicycle-and-the-light/

An interactive presentation of what a fast-moving object actually looks like. It runs in the browser on a desktop or laptop (WebGL 2; Chrome, Safari or Edge), with nothing to install.

It covers these topics in order:
1. Galilean invariance and Maxwell's electromagnetic light, 1865.
2. The Michelson–Morley experiment, 1887, and the fringes it should have seen.
3. FitzGerald, Lorentz and Poincaré, then Einstein, 1905.
4. A magnet and a coil, and what each observer measures.
5. Clocks, length contraction, and light travel time.
6. Gamow's cyclist, and Penrose–Terrell rotation of a sphere and a cube.
7. Zahn's rolling wheel, and Doppler shifts and brightness.
8. Two eyes, the 2025 TU Wien photograph, and the six-face truck.

Use **Next** or the → key to step through. Slides with sliders and buttons can be explored live. Every picture is computed: aberration, Doppler shift, light travel time and Lorentz contraction, solved for each point.

Part of *Speed of Light City* by Christian Sasse. One self-contained file (`index.html`).

Credits: the rolling-wheel slide is based on Corvin Zahn's "Rolling Wheels" (2002, spacetimetravel.org); the reference image shown there is under CC BY-SA 2.0 DE, as credited in the slide. The TU Wien slides follow Hornof, Helm, Juffmann and Schattschneider, *Communications Physics* 8, 161 (2025).
